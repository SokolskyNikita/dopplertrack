# Why Oyster Bay's event 2 fails the search gates (2026-11-03)

**Question.** After `fit`'s objective changed in f890572, the README's Oyster Bay search no longer found the true location ([issue #1](https://github.com/mjshiggins/dopplertrack/issues/1)). What goes wrong, and is the objective to blame?

**Setup.** At the known location, the true flights give the heard gap between the two events and the f0 each event's trajectory needs. Three fits of each event are compared with that truth:
- the committed fits, made with the pre-f890572 brightness objective;
- the current contrast objective, in the README's windows;
- `detect`'s own fits.

Then the best straight-pass curve constrained to the ADS-B speed and distance is compared with the free fits, under both objectives. Development data.

| Fit of event 2 | f0 against what its trajectory needs | Time between the events against the truth |
|---|---|---|
| committed (pre-f890572 brightness objective) | +5 % | 5.0 s short |
| current contrast objective, README windows | +10 % | 9.0 s short |
| `detect` | +11 % | 10.1 s short |

- The search gates are ±6 s on the time between the events and a ±5 % sweep of each f0. The committed fit got through by 1 s, at the edge of the f0 sweep.
- The constrained straight pass, at the true speed and distance, fits event 2's window worse than any of the three free fits, under both objectives.
- At the eagle-cam nest the committed events also miss the ±6 s gate, by 0.2 s. The top candidate, 917 m away, is where the geometry makes up the difference.

**Outcome.** The discrepancy persists under both objectives tested, so changing the objective alone won't fix it. Several causes remain possible:
- the window;
- the scoring;
- the single optimiser start in the constrained fit;
- the trajectory and clock assumptions behind the reference f0.

PR #2 made the old objective `fit`'s default again, which restores the committed fits; `detect`'s path still fails (PLAN gap E1). [2026-11-03-timing-conventions](2026-11-03-timing-conventions.md) explains the size of the timing errors.
